About FrankenNAM

The NAM’s own model code, run on cloud hardware, started from the GFS instead of the NAM’s data assimilation. Same files, same names, stated limits. A community project.

The design

  • The NAM's own code. NMMB/NEMS (the dynamical core and model), NPS (the preprocessor) and UPP (the post-processor), built from the operational NAM v4.2 source. Run on AWS Graviton (arm64) Spot instances; the port needed a small set of source patches, all kept in the repository.
  • Same grid and physics. The full 12 km North American parent domain (954×835, 60 levels, 25 s time step), Ferrier-Aligo microphysics, BMJ convection, MYJ boundary layer, Noah land surface, RRTM radiation. Output is regridded to grid 218 and written as awphys files that match the operational file inventory record for record (454 records at f24).
  • Free maps and free files. The maps are free. The GRIB2 files for the re-run cases are free too, on the Grids page. What it took to run them is on the Costs page.

Strategies we tried

There is more than one way to bring the NAM back. Each one below is a choice about the starting state, the chips or the code. The numbers are on the Validation page or in the repository’s docs/. Most come from one case, 2026-11-01 00Z, so none of this is settled.

(a) Start from the GFS analysis at C: the cold start

The simplest start: take the GFS analysis valid at the cycle time and run the NAM from it. We call this the cold start (the analysis-start arm). Its f00 is the GFS analysis, which is clean, but the NAM’s boundary layer and land state have had no time to settle, and its soil is the GFS’s. On the 2026-11-01 case it was slightly closer to the NAM than the chosen start at f24 for MSLP, and further away at f84 (MSLP 3.87 against 3.45 hPa RMSE). On one winter case, Snowcrete, it trailed the chosen start at every lead. One or two cases.

(b) Start 6 h early with a NAM spin-up (tm06): chosen

Start from the GFS analysis 6 h before the cycle and let the NAM’s physics run for 6 h. Forecast hour 0 is then the model’s own state, not the GFS’s. This is what the products use. On the 2026-11-01 case it was far closer to the archived NAM than the GFS at f24 (MSLP 0.97 against 1.72 hPa RMSE). The cost is that it knows nothing after the start time, and it has a small warm bias in 2 m temperature at f00 that is gone by f24.

(c) Seed the NAM’s land state, and fix a soil bug

The GFS soil is about 0.05 m3/m3 wetter than the NAM’s, so we seed the soil from the NAM’s own state. At f00 the soil moisture is then 0.012 from the NAM’s, against 0.088 for the GFS. In the hindcast we found that frozen ground was missing: the NAM keeps most of the water in cold soil frozen, and our runs started with almost none. It traced to a bug in NOAA’s own nemsinterp, which wrote total soil water where the model expects liquid water. We patched it (patch nps-02). In one check run on the CAD ice-storm case, the frozen share stayed at the NAM’s through f84 (0.582 against 0.582), and the 2 m temperature error against stations fell from 3.42 to 3.04 K, where the NAM’s is 3.01. The rerun of the five hindcast cases with the fix is in progress; until it is done the winter scores are one case.

(d) x86 against Graviton

The same model, built for AWS Graviton (arm64) as well as x86. An 84 h run cost $2.3 on a Graviton Spot node against $3.80 on x86, about 40 % less. Graviton was 6 % slower per forecast hour (200 against 189 s) in single runs on different node classes. The fields differ from the x86 run by roundoff that grows to a few tenths of a kelvin or less by f84, the ordinary sensitivity of a weather model to rounding. Details are on Costs.

(e) WOOF-NAM’s GPU rewrite

WOOF-NAM, from Recast Systems, rewrites the NMMB dynamics and NAM physics for NVIDIA GPUs, in single precision. Its grids are currently free, and that matters: it is the simplest way to get a NAM-like forecast every day. We ran it once ourselves on 2026-11-01 00Z with its own settings. It took 59 minutes on one GPU, and on that case it was the closest to the archived NAM at f84. One case, and a different project’s own choices of start and boundaries.

(f) WOOF-NAM behind our pipeline, with our seed

We built it once: WOOF-NAM’s engine, our 6 h-early start, our NAM soil seed, and our product writer. It ran in 54 minutes from boot to the last file. On the same case it was faster than the CPU run and closer to the NAM at f84 (MSLP 2.08 against 3.45 hPa RMSE). The files match the CPU files record for record except for the records WOOF-NAM does not write, among them 12 h snowfall and the precipitation-type flags. For snow and ice that is the whole point of the NAM, so this path is backlogged until those exist. One case.

The FrankenNAM analysis

A product cycle C (00Z or 12Z) is a 90 h integration that starts at C−6 h. The initial condition is the GFS analysis for that time, the lateral boundaries are the GFS forecasts that follow, and the first 6 h are a NAM spin-up. Integration hour 6 is forecast hour 0 of the product, and hour 90 is f84. That f00 is what FrankenNAM calls its analysis, in the same way the operational NAM’s 6 h catch-up ended in its analysis; it comes from the model alone, not from observations. Because the run starts 6 h early, the first day of forecast can be ready before the cycle time itself. The soil (moisture, temperature, snow water) is seeded from the NAM's own land state rather than the GFS's, because the GFS is about 0.05 m3/m3 wetter than the NAM over CONUS. There is no data assimilation.

The honesty line, on every product. Initialized from the (C−6)Z GFS analysis with a 6 h NAM spin-up. No observations after (C−6)Z. Not the operational NAM.

? what is seeded and what is not
Seeded from the NAM land state at C−6: four soil moisture and temperature layers, skin temperature (over land only), snow water equivalent. Not seeded: canopy water (the model starts it at zero, as the operational cold start did), snow depth (derived from the water equivalent), sea ice and sea-surface temperature (GFS). The seam at the edge of grid 218 is a hard edge: NAM soil inside, GFS soil outside.

What a live cycle would take

FrankenNAM does not run on a schedule, and this is not a service promise. It is what a live 12Z cycle would take, as measured and projected. Measured on the acceptance run (one 32-core Graviton node): 193 s per forecast hour, and the last product file lands 85 s after the model ends. Projected for one such node, launched when the 06Z GFS is on AWS:

C−1:30f00 C−0:10f24 C+1:10f48 C+3:05f84 (the operational NAM's f84 landed at C+2:42)

The pace is measured; the launch time and the clock are projected, because no live cycle has run. A larger node may close the 23 minute gap; that is not yet measured.

Cost per run

A run is about five hours on one Graviton Spot node and costs a few dollars. The measured numbers, the price table for x86 and Graviton and the build are on the Costs page.

? why the first hours differ
The operational analysis blends the previous forecast with observations; FrankenNAM starts from the GFS analysis and lets the NAM physics spin up for 6 h. At f00 that costs a little fidelity to the NAM (see Validation); by f24 the model's own boundary layer and land state matter more than the start. One case does not settle that; the hindcast cases on the Validation page are the test.

How it came back

NCEP announced in September 2026 that the NAM source would be removed on retirement and available only on request. When NCEP's own copy came down, the NAM developers' public GitHub repositories (the model source and the Rocoto workflow) supplied what was needed. The static files the model reads at run time (terrain, land-use and soil tables, CO2) were recovered from Wayback Machine captures of earlier NAM v4.2 releases. In a first run on AWS, the full domain was integrated for 84 h from a historical cycle; its fields tracked the archived NAM closely enough to build a product on. Then the spin-up design, the land seeding and the NAM-identical file layout followed. The strategies above are what we compared on the way.

What could not come back is the NAM's data assimilation: its analysis needs an 80-member ensemble that was never public. That is the honest difference, stated on every product.
