About FrankenNAM

The NAM's own model code, run on cloud hardware, started from the GFS instead of the NAM's data assimilation. Same files, same names, stated limits.

The design

  • The NAM's own code. NMMB/NEMS (the dynamical core and model), NPS (the preprocessor) and UPP (the post-processor), built from the operational NAM v4.2 source. Run on AWS Graviton (arm64) Spot instances; the port needed a small set of source patches, all kept in the repository.
  • Same grid and physics. The full 12 km North American parent domain (954×835, 60 levels, 25 s time step), Ferrier-Aligo microphysics, BMJ convection, MYJ boundary layer, Noah land surface, RRTM radiation. Output is regridded to grid 218 and written as awphys files that match the operational file inventory record for record (454 records at f24).
  • Free maps, paid files. The maps are free. The GRIB2 files are on the Get Operational Data page, and the costs are on the costs page.

The tm06 cycle

A product cycle C (00Z or 12Z) is a 90 h integration that starts at C−6 h. The initial condition is the GFS analysis for that time, the lateral boundaries are the GFS forecasts that follow, and the first 6 h are a NAM spin-up. Integration hour 6 is forecast hour 0 of the product, and hour 90 is f84. The soil (moisture, temperature, snow water) is seeded from the NAM's own land state rather than the GFS's, because the GFS is about 0.05 m3/m3 wetter than the NAM over CONUS. There is no data assimilation.

The honesty line, on every product. Initialized from the (C−6)Z GFS analysis with a 6 h NAM spin-up. No observations after (C−6)Z. Not the operational NAM.

? what is seeded and what is not
Seeded from the NAM land state at C−6: four soil moisture and temperature layers, skin temperature (over land only), snow water equivalent. Not seeded: canopy water (the model starts it at zero, as the operational cold start did), snow depth (derived from the water equivalent), sea ice and sea-surface temperature (GFS). The seam at the edge of grid 218 is a hard edge: NAM soil inside, GFS soil outside.

Delivery clock

Measured on the acceptance run (one 32-core Graviton node): 193 s per forecast hour, and the last product file lands 85 s after the model ends. Projected for a live 12Z cycle on one such node, launched when the 06Z GFS is on AWS:

C−1:30f00 C−0:10f24 C+1:10f48 C+3:05f84 (the operational NAM's f84 landed at C+2:42)

The pace is measured; the launch time and the clock are projected until live cycles run. A larger node may close the 23 minute gap; that is not yet measured.

Cost per run

About $2.60 per cycle: roughly 5.0 h on one m8g.8xlarge Spot instance at about $0.52 an hour (measured on the acceptance run), or about $160 a month for two cycles a day. The whole site, with storage and file hosting, is on the costs page.

? why the first hours differ
The operational analysis blends the previous forecast with observations; FrankenNAM starts from the GFS analysis and lets the NAM physics spin up for 6 h. At f00 that costs a little fidelity to the NAM (see Validation); by f24 the model's own boundary layer and land state matter more than the start. One case does not settle that; the hindcast cases on the Validation page are the test.

How it came back

NCEP announced in September 2026 that the NAM source would be removed on retirement and available only on request. When NCEP's own copy came down, the NAM developers' public GitHub repositories (the model source and the Rocoto workflow) supplied what was needed. The static files the model reads at run time (terrain, land-use and soil tables, CO2) were recovered from Wayback Machine captures of earlier NAM v4.2 releases. In a first run on AWS, the full domain was integrated for 84 h from a historical cycle; its fields tracked the archived NAM closely enough to build a product on. Then the spin-up design, the land seeding and the NAM-identical file layout followed.

What could not come back is the NAM's data assimilation: its analysis needs an 80-member ensemble that was never public. That is the honest difference, stated on every product.
